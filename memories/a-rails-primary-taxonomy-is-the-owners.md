*"the learning rail item should have been part of work and not a new category"* — the Learning surface (the measure → plan → teach loop: the lesson, its graph, the practice, the history) shipped in its own fifth rail group, `learning`, and the owner named it as a category that should not exist: the row belongs in **work**, the group the conversation, inbox, design, sessions, huddles and desk live in.

**Why:** the owner opens a lesson the way he opens a chat — learning is work, not a shelf of knowledge to browse. The rail's four groups (`work`, `intelligence`, `knowledge`, `system`) are the product's primary taxonomy; growing a group for every new feature turns the rail into a feature index, which is the shape the owner's rail research ruled against (one flat level, chunked, never a tier). A new row is a placement decision, and placement is the owner's, because the grouping is how the product says what each surface is.

**How to apply:**
- A new surface lands as a **row in an existing group** by default. Name the group you chose and why in the delivery line; do not invent a group with one row in it (`['learning', 'learning', [['learning', 'learning']]]` was the shape the owner rejected).
- The learning case's reasoning, kept for the next one like it: "work the owner opens the way they open a chat" → work; "a shelf you browse after the work" → knowledge. A loop-style feature belongs with the work, not with the inventory.
- A group's mark is only needed when the group has one: moving a lone row into a group makes the group's own icon unused and the row's own icon take its place — keep one drawing per concept.
- The rail's shape is pinned: the harness's group count, the destination count and the "every link sits in its group" check all move together, and the surfaces doc's inventory (`fifteen destinations in four groups`) is part of the same change.
